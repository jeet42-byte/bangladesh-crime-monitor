# Draft email to UIU supervisor: ethics question for Study 1

**To:** [supervisor] · **Subject:** Ethics approval needed? Passive email-security census of Bangladeshi institutions

Dear [Sir/Madam],

I am preparing a short quantitative study for journal submission and would like your advice on whether it needs formal ethics approval, or whether a written exemption is enough. I would also welcome you as a co-author if you are interested.

**Study:** a census of email-spoofing protection (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) and basic website security (HTTPS, TLS version, security headers) for 322 Bangladeshi institutions:
- banks and mobile financial services
- ministries and national agencies
- public and private universities

**Method (passive only):**
- reading **public DNS records**, which are published so that any mail server in the world can read them
- **one ordinary visit** to each institution's public homepage, the same as any browser
- **no** vulnerability scanning, password attempts, form submissions or access to any system
- no personal data is collected; the units are institutions, not people

**Safeguards:**
- the protocol is pre-registered on OSF before data collection
- results are published **only in aggregate**, by sector and ownership type
- **no institution is named** as weak
- spoofable banks and government bodies are **notified privately at least 60 days before submission**, through BGD e-GOV CIRT (and Bangladesh Bank for banks), with simple guidance on how to fix the issue

Similar passive census studies have recently been published for other countries (Iraq's higher-education sector, 2026; the Maldives, *Future Internet*, 2026), and the design follows the Menlo Report principles for ICT research.

The protocol is attached. Could you let me know whether UIU requires an ethics application for this kind of study, or whether a short written confirmation from you would be sufficient?
